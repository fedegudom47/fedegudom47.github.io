The Flint Water Crisis

@cr-section

In 2015, water samples were collected from homes across Flint, Michigan to test for lead contamination.

Each droplet represents one water sample from a Flint home.

The EPA set an action level of 15 parts per billion (ppb) for lead.

Water systems must keep 90% of samples below this threshold.

But there was a problem. Two samples with high lead levels were removed from the official data.

After removing these samples, the 90th percentile dropped significantly—making the water appear safer than it was.

@cr-section

The Impact

The official 90th percentile was reported as 11.4 ppb, just below the EPA action level.

But with all samples included, it was actually 18 ppb—well above the safe threshold.

This data manipulation helped hide the severity of Flint’s water crisis, leading to prolonged exposure of residents to dangerous lead levels.
